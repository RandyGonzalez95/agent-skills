# Presentation Design

Establish the audience, decision, presentation setting, source material, and output format. Preserve a supplied template and actual commercial scope. Do not convert every presentation into a sales pitch.

## Build the story

For each slide, define its narrative job, what the audience should understand afterward, the supporting evidence, and the next question. Sequence slides so the argument accumulates. A proposal may cover problem, diagnosis, approach, deliverables, evidence, price, boundaries, responsibilities, and next decision; choose the order for the actual buyer.

Distinguish targets from promises and candidate terms from approved ones. Do not invent prices, case studies, performance data, or scope commitments. Simplify content before shrinking type.

## Design and produce

Use the actual brand or template, with consistent typography, color roles, spacing, and charts. Vary visual energy and layout where the story benefits. Do not mandate centered text, emotional arcs, one chart library, or a particular font for all decks.

For HTML decks, provide usable navigation, stable layout, and a workable export or viewing route. For PowerPoint or native Google/Figma Slides, use the available format-specific skill/tool and its verification requirements. A flattened image deck is not fully editable slide content.

Use charts suited to the comparison, showing sources, units, labels, and meaningful baselines. Avoid decorative charts or invented numbers. Keep important meaning available beyond color alone.

## Verify

Render and inspect every slide. Check contrast at the actual text location, including across gradients and images; use a stable backing surface where necessary. Check footnotes and small labels as carefully as titles.

Fix clipping, overflow, unintended overlap, missing fonts/assets, misleading charts, and broken narrative transitions. Deliver the requested file or viewing link, editable source when produced, and material unresolved decisions. State which rendering and interaction checks actually ran.
