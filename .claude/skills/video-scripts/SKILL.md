# Video Scripts

Establish the viewer, desired takeaway, available evidence, duration, and production constraints. Use the supplied material first. If no experiment has run, write a proposed experiment in future tense rather than a completed case study.

## Choose the structure

- **Instructional tip:** specific problem, concrete action, important adjustment, why it helps, and credible consequence. Use flexible wording rather than fixed opening and closing phrases.
- **Completed experiment:** situation, friction, test, observed result, and decision.
- **Experiment announcement:** question, stakes, test, success criterion, and how the result will be evaluated.
- **Personal or founder story:** actual moment, tension, choice, consequence, and relevance to the viewer.

For a 20-second tip, roughly 45 to 55 spoken words is a starting estimate; for 30 to 45 seconds, roughly 65 to 100. Adapt to pauses, demonstrations, and the speaker. Count the final spoken words and label runtime as estimated unless timed aloud.

## Write for speech and evidence

Open with the actual tension and deliver the promised payoff. Keep one main idea, concrete actions, and a natural cadence. Preserve the speaker's supplied voice without inventing emotion, dialogue, client experiences, or expertise.

Separate observed output, editorial judgment, and measured business results. An improved draft does not establish improved conversion; drafting speed excludes review time unless measured. Verify current tool behavior before scripting precise product instructions.

## Deliver

Return one uninterrupted spoken script plus aligned shot cues, separate on-screen text, and a caption when requested or useful. If the user asks for script only, provide that. Keep shots feasible with the actual assets and filming setup. Label illustrative AI visuals so they do not masquerade as evidence.

Review hook/payoff alignment, factual support, timing, and shot feasibility. Writing a script does not run its experiment, generate paid footage, or publish the result.
