# Presentation Design

Establish audience, decision, setting, sources, and output format. Preserve supplied templates and commercial scope; use a sales narrative only when appropriate.

## Story and design

Give each slide a narrative job, takeaway, evidence, and next question. Sequence the argument for the audience. Proposals may cover problem, approach, deliverables, proof, price, boundaries, responsibilities, and next decision.

Separate targets from promises and proposed terms from approvals. Use real prices, case studies, data, and commitments. Simplify content before shrinking type.

Follow the brand/template with consistent type, color roles, spacing, and charts; vary layouts purposefully. Chart comparisons need sources, units, labels, meaningful baselines, and meaning beyond color.

## Produce and verify

HTML decks need usable navigation, stable layout, and a viewing/export route. For PowerPoint or native Slides, use available format-specific tools and verification guidance. Flattened images are not fully editable slides.

Render every slide. Check contrast on actual backgrounds, small labels, clipping, overlap, fonts/assets, chart accuracy, and narrative transitions. Deliver requested files/links, editable source when produced, unresolved decisions, and the rendering/interaction checks performed.
